**house:119:1:89: Requiring a Vietnam-theater veteran cancer study, tracking and pension-limit extension**

H.R.586 as amended would require VA, consulting CDC, to commence within 120 days after enactment an epidemiological study of cholangiocarcinoma in veterans who served in the Vietnam theater during the Vietnam era, using the VA Central Cancer Registry and National Program of Cancer Registries. It would identify incidence in covered veterans and U.S. residents from the beginning of that era to enactment, with the listed age, gender, race, ethnicity and diagnosis-location breakdowns. The incorporated dated section 101(29) definition distinguishes November 1, 1955-May 7, 1975 for Republic-of-Vietnam service from August 5, 1964-May 7, 1975 in other cases; this does not determine an individual's service classification.
An initial report of results and administrative/legislative recommendations would be due within one year after study completion. Follow-up reports would be periodic as determined by the Secretary; VA must continue tracking through its registry and provide that information in follow-ups. The 120-day clock is for commencement, not completion. No fixed study-completion deadline, causal cancer finding, new service-connection presumption or treatment entitlement follows.
The same choice would extend the existing 38 U.S.C.5503(d)(7) expiration from November 30 to December 31, 2031. The dated subsection limits pension to $90 monthly after the admission month for a veteran with neither spouse nor child whose nursing-facility services are covered by Medicaid; corresponding rules apply to a surviving spouse with no child and a child entitled under section 1542. The facility definition excludes a State home receiving the specified VA per-diem payments. Medicaid facility payments cannot be reduced by the permitted pension; excess-payment recovery retains the willful-concealment qualification. Administration is paid from available compensation/pension amounts. This extends those conditional rules by one month; it does not newly impose a universal pension cap.

**house:119:1:115: Commissioning a fixed-wing aircrew cancer study with a pension-limit extension**

H.R.530 as amended would require VA to seek a National Academies agreement within 30 days after enactment for a study of cancer prevalence and mortality among covered active-duty fixed-wing aircrew. VA must finalize the agreement within 60 days after negotiations begin. If that deadline is missed, VA must report reasons and an estimated finalization date to the two Veterans' Affairs committees and brief them at least every 60 days after the failure. Neither clock guarantees a completed study by a fixed date.
The study would identify occupational exposures, review literature on associations with overall cancer morbidity/mortality and the listed cancers, and determine prevalence/mortality to the extent possible using available data. The list includes brain, colon/rectal, kidney, lung, melanoma skin, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, pancreatic, prostate, the printed 'testiscular', thyroid and urinary bladder cancers, and other cancers the Secretary determines appropriate in consultation with the Academies. This preserves the printed term without diagnosing a person or asserting causation.
Available data may include VA/Defense/individual-Service databases, the national death index, and the study conducted under section 750 of Public Law 116-283. These are optional examples, not a requirement to use every dataset or an adoption of the 2021 study's contents/results. That prior study and its findings have not been separately reviewed for this candidate. At study conclusion the Academies must report results to VA and the two committees. Covered individuals served on active duty in the Army, Navy, Air Force or Marine Corps as fixed-wing aircrew, including pilots, navigators, weapons/aircraft-system operators or other crew who regularly flew fixed-wing aircraft. The definition does not extend to every military occupation or all aircraft types.

H.R.980 as amended would remove the second sentence of 38 U.S.C.3697B(a), requiring on-campus educational/vocational counseling to be supplied by VA employees who provide section 3697A services. The underlying campus counseling duty, selected-location/space/access criteria and reporting remain. The bill inserts no bachelor's-degree condition; such shorthand in supporting remarks is not the operative amendment.
For Chapter 31 rehabilitation, VA may approve a program containing non-degree flight training notwithstanding section 3680A(b)'s ordinary higher-education/degree-credit restriction. This discretionary approval applies to rehabilitation programs approved on or after August 1, 2026. It is not automatic flight-course funding or repeal of all course/program approval conditions. The dated 3104 baseline already lists counseling, training, placement and related rehabilitation services; the bill does not newly create all of them.
VA must establish a dedicated Education Call Center number for Chapter 31 services; each regional office must publish a contact name, phone and email on its website. These are specified contact duties, not a new technology-capacity finding. Extension requests under 3105(c) must be approved OR denied within 30 days after submission. Existing necessary-rehabilitation and disability/occupation-change or serious-employment-handicap conditions remain. Timely decision is not automatic approval. Reports covering requests, approvals and denials are due within one year after enactment and annually thereafter for five years.
The whole choice also extends 5503(d)(7) expiration from January 31 to July 31, 2033. Public Law 119-43, approved December 1, 2025, supplies the January 2033 baseline by amending the earlier November 2031 expiration; the dated 2024 Code alone does not supply it.
