**house:119:2:78: Repealing income-tested home electrification rebates and related energy assistance**

The February 25 House question was passage of H.R.4758, the Homeowner Energy Freedom Act, as engrossed in the House. It also removes the named high-efficiency electric rebate reference from the separate HOMES program's noncombination clause. This is one vote on that whole repeal/rescission package, not three component votes.

Section 50122 funds state and Tribal point-of-sale rebates for qualified household electrification projects. An eligible household has income less than 150 percent of area median income; demonstrated eligibility for another income-restricted federal program is specified as evidence. Eligible entities also include owners of multifamily buildings with at least 50 percent eligible-household residents, and governmental, commercial or nonprofit entities carrying out projects on an eligible household's behalf. Those third parties generally must discount the household's cost by the rebate. This is not a universal homeowner benefit or a medical treatment program.

Qualified projects include new construction, replacement of a nonelectric appliance and first-time purchase of the specified electric appliances. Energy Star certification applies where applicable. The maximum rebate is the lesser of the income-tier cost share and item limits, subject to a cumulative $14,000 cap for each eligible entity. Below 80 percent of area median income the cost share is up to 100 percent; the printed second cost tier is 80 through 150 percent and up to 50 percent, while the separate household eligibility definition says less than 150 percent. Preserve those two printed boundaries without inventing eligibility at exactly 150 percent. Item caps are $1,750 for a heat-pump water heater, $8,000 for a heat pump for space heating/cooling, $840 for an electric stove/cooktop/range/oven or heat-pump clothes dryer, $4,000 for an electric load service center, $1,600 for insulation/air sealing/ventilation, and $2,500 for wiring. These are ceilings, not guaranteed payments. A separate installation incentive of up to $500 for an entity acting on a household's behalf is tied to project scale and exempt from the household-discount rule. The program prohibits combining another federal grant or rebate for the same qualified project. Original appropriations were $4.275 billion for states and $225 million for Tribes through September 30, 2031; those original amounts are not the remaining unobligated balances. Administrative limits and the specific exemption from 10 CFR 420.18 expenditure limits remain part of the targeted program's conditions.

Section 50131 originally provided $330 million for latest-code assistance and $670 million for zero-energy-code assistance through September 30, 2029.
