## Whole-choice distinctions

Each Yea supports passage of the entire named-rule disapproval resolution; each
Nay opposes that disapproval. It does not divide a member's position among the
rule's components or establish motive, a preferred replacement or a general
technology stance. Official Clerk facts are Foushee five Nay and Massie five Yea,
all resolved in-service observations, reusing one shared core per action.

H.J.Res.20's rule distinguishes a stricter branch for specified below2-gallon,
above50000Btu/h consumer heaters from EF-to-UEF conversions for other specified
classes. H.J.Res.24 changes non-display doors and refrigeration systems, with
different compliance dates and AWEF versus AWEF2 metrics; display-door/panel
standards and the medical/scientific/research product exclusion remain distinct.

H.J.Res.42's rule spans multiple product groups and includes new/changed reporting,
obsolete-field removal, sampling/testing/enforcement corrections, labels and
covered-equipment definitions. Initial, annual and standards-linked reporting
dates differ. The walk-in door manufacture-date label is qualified by non-display
doors with foam insulation; compressor break-in requires reported duration and
has a20-hour cap. Clarifying general-service-lamp certification does not create
a new threshold. This is not a vote only on labels or a single appliance.

H.J.Res.75's rule distinguishes old/new manufacture dates, remote/self-contained
equipment, compartment/feature calculations, large-capacity treatment and continuing
exclusions. Its literal "Office of the Office" wording is preserved. H.J.Res.61's
rule adds previously unregulated rubber-processing standards, adopts one final
THC standard rather than the proposal's silica split, preserves metal-HAP/fPM
alternatives and qualified facility averaging, and adds sensing, inspection,
testing/reporting/recordkeeping duties. Existing subcategory limits are not all
new requirements. Existing/new-source clocks and construction-date thresholds
remain separate; projected control installation is not an actual outcome.

House passage is not enactment or a present legal outcome. The meanings describe
the identified published rule versions. They do not certify later delays,
withdrawals, litigation/current-law integration, restored prior standards,
substantially-the-same CRA consequences or actual device/energy/cost/health effects.
The rule's own authority/background is source-attributed context, not an exhaustive
independent statutory/CFR/case-law integration.
