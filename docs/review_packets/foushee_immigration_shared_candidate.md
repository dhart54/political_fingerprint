### S.5: expanding specified immigration detention and State enforcement litigation

**Compact:** Foushee opposed passage of S.5 with those provisions; no preferred alternative is established. S.5 would expand mandatory immigration custody and DHS detainers for the specified inadmissibility/offense combination (burglary, theft, larceny, shoplifting, assault of a law-enforcement officer, or any crime resulting in another person's death or serious bodily injury), and add harm-conditioned State suits over specified detention, release, parole and visa duties.

**Detail:**

**house:119:1:23**

Foushee opposed passage of S.5 with those provisions; no preferred alternative is established.

S.5 would extend the federal mandatory-custody category to the following offenses: burglary, theft, larceny, shoplifting, assault of a law-enforcement officer, or any crime resulting in another person's death or serious bodily injury. The listed property-offense terms, assault of a law-enforcement officer and serious bodily injury take the meanings assigned by the jurisdiction where the acts occurred. The exact bill adds an INA 236(c) mandatory-custody category requiring inadmissibility under INA 212(a)(6)(A), (6)(C), or (7) together with a specified criminal predicate: being charged, arrested, convicted, admitting commission, or admitting acts constituting the essential elements. These inadmissibility categories concern presence without admission/parole or undesignated arrival, material immigration fraud/false citizenship claims, and specified entry-document defects, subject to their existing statutory qualifications. DHS must issue a detainer for a person meeting the new ground and, if the person is not otherwise detained by Federal, State or local officials, effectively and expeditiously take custody. The bill also gives a State attorney general or other authorized State officer standing, on alleged harm to the State or residents (including financial harm in excess of $100), to seek appropriate injunctive relief in district court over five specified federal matters: INA 235(b)(1)/(2) detention/removal requirements; INA 236 release, bond or parole decisions; the existing INA 243(d) visa-discontinuance requirement for countries denying or unreasonably delaying acceptance; violation of the existing INA 212(d)(5)(A) case-by-case urgent-humanitarian/significant-public-benefit parole limits; and INA 241(a)(2) detention during the removal period. Courts must advance and expedite these cases to the greatest practicable extent. INA 236(e) removes its express release/grant shield while leaving the other printed discretionary-judgment/detention/revocation/denial restrictions, and INA 242(f)(1) receives an exception for the printed listed actions, while its separate final-removal-order injunction standard remains. These review changes do not establish a general right to enjoin every immigration action. The parole clause changes its named administering actor to the Secretary of Homeland Security. The baseline removal period is generally 90 days with specified starting/extension conditions; the bill adds litigation standing over its detention requirement rather than creating that period. The pre-existing narrow witness/cooperator-protection release exception remains, redesignated as INA 236(c)(4).
