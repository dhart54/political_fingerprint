### H.R.4638 federal police-animal immigration grounds

**Compact:** Foushee opposed passage of H.R.4638 with those grounds; no preferred alternative is established. H.R.4638 would add an inadmissibility ground for the specified federal police-animal offense and a matching deportability ground within the admitted-person framework; both permit conviction or specified admissions of conduct.

**Detail:**

**house:119:2:96**

Foushee opposed passage of H.R.4638 with those grounds; no preferred alternative is established.

The exact H.R.4638 EH proposal adds INA 212(a)(2)(J) inadmissibility and 237(a)(2)(G) deportability for an alien convicted of, admitting commission of, or admitting acts constituting the essential elements of an offense under 18 USC 1368. Both new clauses allow those specified admissions, despite the conviction-only phrasing in the bill title. Arrest alone is not the printed predicate. The incorporated 1368 offense already criminalizes willfully and maliciously harming a police animal, including attempts and conspiracies. Police animal means a dog or horse employed by a Federal agency in any branch primarily to aid criminal detection, law enforcement or apprehension. It is not every working animal or every State/local police animal, and the bill is not limited to CBP animals. The existing offense carries fine and up-to 1year imprisonment, with up-to 10 years for permanent disability/disfigurement, serious bodily injury or death; the immigration clauses do not add an injury/penalty-length threshold. The bill adds INA consequences to that existing crime, not a new animal-harm offense or a claim that all covered conduct previously had no immigration consequences. Existing INA conviction rules/other criminal grounds remain. Their enumerated petty-offense,212(h) and pardon exceptions are not written as exceptions to newJ/G; discretionary temporary-admission/T/U waiver authorities remain within their own scope. No blanket relief or automatic deportation is inferred. The new 237(a)(2) deportability ground sits within the existing 237(a) framework for an alien (including a crewman) in and admitted to the United States, with removal upon the specified government order. It is not a universal new deportability ground for every unadmitted alien. INA 101 distinguishes immigration admission (lawful entry after inspection/authorization) from admitting criminal conduct; parole alone is not admission. This governing status scope is separate from any offense's after-admission timing element. No individual admission status or actual removal order is inferred.

The Clerk recorded the House result as 'Passed' on 2026-03-19. This does not establish enactment.

- INA 237(a) deportability concerns an alien in and admitted to the United States, subject to the governing order framework. Immigration admission and admissions of criminal conduct are different conditions; parole alone is not immigration admission. The new numbered ground does not discard this governing chapeau or necessarily add an after-admission-crime timing element.
- Both new INA grounds allow conviction or specified admissions; neither requires conviction exclusively or follows from arrest alone. The incorporated offense requires willful and malicious harm, attempt or conspiracy.
- The animal is a federally employed dog or horse with the specified principal criminal/law-enforcement purpose, regardless of Federal branch. All animals, all State/local police animals and only-CBP animals are unsupported expansions/narrowings.
- 18 USC 1368 is pre-existing. Its aggravated-harm prison ceiling is not an eligibility threshold in these new INA clauses; existing INA exceptions/waivers keep their own scope rather than universally attaching to newJ/G.
- This complete bill passage choice establishes no component preference, animal injury, guilt, visa/admission denial, removal or enactment.
