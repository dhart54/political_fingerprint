## Later-captured baseline handoff

Reuse the completed checkpoint 192 primary/floor and Privacy Act/Judicial Redress research. Its remaining HIPAA dependency now has useful **existing checkpoint 223 captures**:

- **H1/H2:**45 CFR 160.103's covered-entity categories, the electronic-transaction qualification for a health-care provider, and complete health-plan inclusion/exclusion definition. Medicaid and named other health plans appear; the government-funded-program exclusion has its own exceptions and principal-purpose/activity predicates. Do not equate every agency administering one of the bill's programs with a HIPAA-covered entity.
- **H3/H4:**HIPAA “individual” is the person who is the subject of PHI; PHI's complete media alternatives and education/employment/deceased-person exclusions remain. This wording differs from Privacy Act 552a(a)(2)'s citizen/LPR definition in A1. The distinction is a source check, not a universal-coverage or all-other-person-remedies ruling.
- **H5:**The dated 164.512(f) law-enforcement provision is conditional. Its administrative-request branch requires a response required by law **and** relevance/materiality, a specific/limited scope and inability reasonably to use de-identified information. Other branches have their own identification-data exclusions, victim-consent/emergency predicates, decedent/premises conditions and emergency/domestic-violence qualifications. A written request alone must not be presented as an unconditional HIPAA disclosure license. Whether/how a proposed request fits a branch remains a legal application for review.
- **A2–A4/J1–J2:**Keep Privacy Act disclosure exceptions, rule-dependent exemptions and the Judicial Redress Act's covered-record/person/country/designated-agency conditions and limited remedies. A1 alone does not establish all privacy protections or absolute remedy exclusion for everyone else; this packet supplies no current designation or individual entitlement determination.
